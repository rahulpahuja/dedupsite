A phone can feel full only months after you bought it. Usually nothing is wrong: several kinds of data simply keep growing in the background. Knowing which ones helps you clean up the right things.

## Where the space goes

- **Photos and videos.** Modern cameras take large photos, and video at high resolution or frame rate uses far more space per minute than photos do.
- **Messaging apps.** Chat apps save every image, video, voice note and document you receive, often duplicating media that is already in your gallery.
- **App data.** Apps store offline maps, downloaded music, podcasts and video for offline viewing. These count against your storage even though they don't appear in the gallery.
- **Cache.** Apps keep temporary files to load faster. Cache can be cleared, but it usually builds up again as you use the app.
- **System and updates.** Android itself, its updates and preinstalled apps take a fixed part of storage that you can't free.
- **Duplicates and leftovers.** Repeated downloads, old APK install files, and copies of the same photo or video in several folders.

## How to check what is using space

1. Open **Settings → Storage** on your phone. Android groups usage by category, such as images, videos, apps and system. The exact names vary by manufacturer.
2. Tap a category to see which apps or files are largest.
3. For apps, open **Settings → Apps**, choose an app, then **Storage** to see its data and cache separately.

## What to clean first

1. **Duplicate and large videos.** The biggest savings per file. See [how to find duplicate videos](/duplicate-video-cleaner).
2. **Chat media.** Duplicates and large files in WhatsApp and similar apps. See the [WhatsApp cleaner](/whatsapp-cleaner).
3. **Duplicate and similar photos.** Burst shots and repeated saves. See the [duplicate photo cleaner](/duplicate-photo-cleaner).
4. **Old downloads and APK files** you no longer need.
5. **App cache**, from each app's storage settings, if an app's cache has grown very large.

## Keep it manageable

Back up photos and videos you want to keep, limit automatic media downloads in chat apps, and check storage every month or two rather than waiting for the "storage full" warning.
