A storage cleaner has to look at your photos to find duplicates, and your photo library is about as personal as data gets. Where that analysis happens matters.

## On-device versus cloud analysis

With **cloud analysis**, photos, or data derived from them, are uploaded to a server that does the comparing. That can be fast, but your images leave your control, and their privacy then depends on the server's security and the company's policies.

With **on-device analysis**, the comparison runs on your phone's own processor. The photos are read locally and the results stay on the phone. Nothing about the content of your pictures needs to be sent anywhere.

## What DeDup analyses on your phone

- **Duplicate detection.** Checksums and visual fingerprints are calculated on the phone and stored in its local database.
- **Best-shot suggestions.** A sharpness measure and a face detector run on the phone to suggest which photo in a group to keep.
- **Photo sorting.** Google ML Kit's on-device image labelling sorts recent photos into groups such as screenshots, memes and receipts.
- **Photo search** matches your words against album, app and date information, also on the phone.

DeDup's privacy policy states that your photos, videos, files, contacts and their names or metadata are not uploaded. On Android, scan results are kept in an encrypted database on the phone.

## What does leave the phone

"On-device" applies to the analysis, not to everything an app does. DeDup, like most apps, sends some data:

- **Crash reports and usage analytics** through Firebase, to fix bugs and see which features are used.
- **Your name and email**, if you sign in with Google, so you stay signed in. Signing in is needed to delete files.
- **Feedback** you choose to send.

The [privacy policy](/privacy) lists all of it. You can delete your account in the app's settings.

## How to check any app's privacy claims

- Read the **Data safety** section of the Google Play listing.
- Read the privacy policy for what is sent and to whom.
- Check the permissions Android asks for, and deny ones that don't match what the app does.
- Be wary of claims like "100% offline" from apps that also need an account or show ads.
